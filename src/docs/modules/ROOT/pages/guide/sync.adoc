= Cloud sync
Paul Snow

:description: How cross-device sync works in RepFoundry, how to turn it on, and what happens when the same workout is edited on two devices.

RepFoundry can keep your workout data in step across your own devices using
your phone's cloud account. It is entirely optional, off by default, and
built so that a sync failure never gets in the way of logging a set.

image::sync.png["Cloud sync switched on in Settings, showing the last sync time, a Sync Now action, and an option to disable sync and delete the cloud copy",380]

NOTE: This screen is the same on Android. What differs is where your data
goes — iCloud on iOS, Google Drive on Android — and that is not something the
settings screen shows.

== What sync does and does not do

When sync is turned on, RepFoundry copies your workout data to your cloud
account and pulls down anything newer from your other devices. It runs
automatically after you finish a workout and when you reopen the app, with
no progress bar and no interruption — you can keep training while it happens
in the background.

Sync does *not* create a live, real-time connection between devices. Each
device only sees another device's changes the next time it syncs, and there
is no dedicated RepFoundry server in the middle — your data goes straight to
your own cloud storage and nowhere else.

== Which cloud service your platform uses

RepFoundry uses each platform's own cloud service rather than a server it
runs itself:

* *iOS* syncs via iCloud.
* *Android* syncs via Google Drive, using a private storage area that does
  not appear alongside your other Drive files.

This has one important consequence: *sync does not cross platforms.* Two
iPhones on the same iCloud account can sync with each other, and two Android
phones on the same Google account can sync with each other, but an iPhone
and an Android phone cannot sync with each other at all — they write to
completely separate cloud stores. See xref:platform-differences.adoc[] for
the full picture.

== Turning on sync

Sync lives in *Settings*, under *Cross-Device Sync*:

. Turn on *Enable Cross-Device Sync*.
. A consent dialog explains that your workout data will be saved to your own
  Google Drive or iCloud account, that RepFoundry cannot access it there, and
  that you can delete it at any time. Accept to continue.
. RepFoundry runs an initial sync straight away, which may show your
  platform's sign-in or permission screen. Sync is only switched on once this
  first run succeeds.

Once it's on, the same screen shows when you last synced and offers a
*Sync Now* button for syncing on demand. *Disable Sync & Delete Cloud Data*
turns sync off and removes your data from cloud storage — your data on this
device is not affected.

== When the same data changes on two devices

If you edit the same workout, set, or other record on two devices before
they've had a chance to sync, RepFoundry does not merge the two edits or ask
you to choose. It keeps whichever edit was made *more recently*, and
discards the other one.

This applies per record — so editing a workout's notes on one device and
logging a new set on another affects different records and both changes
survive. But if you change the same set's weight on your phone and then
change it again on your tablet before syncing, only the later change
remains once both devices have synced.

Deleting something is treated the same way: a deletion generally wins over
an edit made before it, so a record you delete on one device stays deleted
even if another device edited it earlier and hasn't synced yet.

== When sync fails

Sync is best-effort by design. If your device is offline, cloud
authentication has expired, or the cloud service is unreachable, an
automatic sync simply does nothing and tries again next time — no error
message appears, because automatic sync is not meant to interrupt you.

Sync started manually with *Sync Now* is the exception: it tells you whether
it succeeded or failed, so you have a way to check when you actually want to
know. Either way, a failed sync never touches or loses the data already on
your device — it just means the other device doesn't have your latest
changes yet.
