= Analytics
Paul Snow
0.0.0
:description: How to read the analytics charts in RepFoundry — weekly volume, muscle balance, personal record timeline, and training load.

The *Analytics* screen turns your logged workouts into charts so you can see
trends that are hard to spot set by set: whether your training volume is
rising or falling, whether one muscle group is being neglected, how your
personal records have progressed, and how hard each week has felt.

Analytics needs a few completed workouts before it has anything to show. On a
new profile, or one with very little history, the screen shows a short
message instead of the charts.

NOTE: Warm-up sets are left out of every chart and every total on this page.
Only working sets count.

== Weekly volume trend

This chart plots your total training volume — weight lifted multiplied by
reps completed — for each calendar week, as a line. A rising line means
you're doing more overall work week to week; a falling line means less.

Tap or hover a point on the line to see the exact volume for that week and
the percentage change compared with the week before. The very first week
shown has no prior week to compare against, so no percentage change is
displayed for it.

== Muscle group balance

This chart shows what share of your total training volume went to each
muscle group, so you can spot imbalances — for example, a lot of volume on
push exercises and very little on pulling or legs.

Because the chart needs at least three muscle groups with recorded volume to
draw a meaningful shape, it will ask you to train a few more muscle groups
before it appears. Cardio activity is tracked separately and is not included
in this breakdown.

== Personal record timeline

This is a scrollable row of cards, one per personal record, with the most
recent record first. Each card shows:

* The exercise
* The kind of record — estimated one-rep max, heaviest weight, most reps, or
  most volume in a single set
* The value achieved
* The date it was achieved

Estimated one-rep max is not a tested maximum — it's a projection calculated
from a set's weight and reps using the Epley formula:

[source,text]
----
estimated 1RM = weight × (1 + reps / 30)
----

Because it's an estimate, treat it as a useful trend line rather than a
number to load onto the bar. For the full list of personal records, see
xref:guide/history.adoc[].

== Weekly training load

Training load combines how much you trained with how hard it felt. Each
week's load is the number of working sets performed multiplied by the
average RPE (rate of perceived exertion) you logged that week. Weeks where
no sets have an RPE logged show a load of zero rather than being left out of
the chart.

Use this alongside the volume trend: rising volume with steady or falling
load can suggest your sessions are feeling easier for the same amount of
work, while rising load can be an early signal to plan a lighter week.

== Where to find it

Analytics is available from the app's main navigation. On larger screens
(tablets, desktop), all four charts and a summary strip of key numbers —
total volume, workout count, average session volume, and PR count — are
shown together on one dashboard. On phones, each chart appears in its own
card as you scroll down the screen.
