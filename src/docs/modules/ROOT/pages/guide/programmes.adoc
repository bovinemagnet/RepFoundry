== What is a programme?

A programme is a named, multi-week training plan. You set how many weeks it
runs for, then assign a saved workout to specific days across those weeks.
Once a programme is under way, starting a workout from it loads the
right workout for today and can automatically nudge your working weights up
(or back down) as the weeks go on.

[TIP]
====
A programme is not the same as a xref:guide/templates.adoc[template]. A
template is a single reusable workout — a list of exercises with target sets
and reps. A programme is the schedule: it decides *which* template you do on
*which* day of *which* week, and adds automatic weight progression on top.
You build templates first, then assign them into a programme.
====

== Building a programme

. Open *Settings* and tap *Programmes*.
. Tap the add button and give the programme a name and a duration in weeks.
. On the programme's schedule grid, tap any day to assign one of your saved
  templates to it, or leave it blank to mark it a rest day. Tap a filled day
  again to remove or replace the assignment.
. Add progression rules for any exercises you want to increase automatically
  (see below).
. Save the programme.

You can come back and edit the name, duration, schedule, or progression
rules at any time. Deleting a programme removes its whole schedule and all
of its progression rules along with it — this cannot be undone.

== How progression works

Progression rules let RepFoundry adjust your suggested working weight for an
exercise automatically as a programme advances, instead of you having to
work out the numbers yourself each week.

For each rule you choose an exercise, a strategy, a value, and how often the
step should happen (every week, every second week, and so on). There are
three strategies:

Add a fixed amount::
Adds a set amount of weight each time the rule is due — for example, add
2.5 kg to the working weight. This suits lifts where you progress in small,
consistent jumps, such as the main barbell lifts.

Increase by a percentage::
Increases the working weight by a percentage each time the rule is due — for
example, add 5%. This suits exercises where a fixed weight jump would be too
big or too small relative to what you're already lifting, such as
accessory or isolation exercises.

Reduce by a percentage (deload)::
Reduces the working weight by a percentage each time the rule is due. Use
this to deliberately schedule a lighter recovery week partway through a
programme, rather than always pushing the weight upward.

The first week of a programme is always the baseline — no progression is
applied yet. From then on, a step is applied at the frequency you chose (for
example, every second week). Each step is calculated from the weight you
actually lifted last time, so increases carry forward on their own; you
don't need to update anything by hand. If an exercise has no progression
rule, RepFoundry just carries your last logged weight forward unchanged, the
same as it does for any workout without a programme.

A weight can never be pushed down to zero — if a reduction would take it
that low, RepFoundry leaves the weight unchanged instead.

== Starting a workout from a programme

From the workout screen, choose *Start from Programme* and pick the
programme you want to follow. RepFoundry looks at the schedule for today's
day of the week within the programme's current week and starts that
workout, with suggested weights already adjusted for any progression that's
due.

If there's nothing scheduled for today — for example, it's a rest day in
the schedule — RepFoundry tells you no workout is scheduled today rather
than starting one.

A programme's week count begins the first time you actually start a workout
from it, not when you created it. So you can build a programme in advance
and it will still begin at week one whenever you first use it.
