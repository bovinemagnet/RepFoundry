Workout logging is the core of RepFoundry: recording sets with weight, reps, and optional RPE against exercises drawn from a library, with automatic personal record detection along the way.

image::workout-logging.png["A bench press card part way through a session: sets one and two logged at 55 kg and 60 kg, a third typed in at 62.5 kg for 6 reps, and the Log Set button ready",380]

If you have not logged a workout yet, start with xref:first-workout.adoc[] for a start-to-finish beginner walkthrough. This page is the fuller reference — everything else the feature can do.

== The exercise library

RepFoundry ships with 21 built-in exercises covering common barbell, dumbbell, machine, and bodyweight movements. You can add unlimited custom exercises of your own from the exercise picker. For the full library reference — searching, editing, and the default exercise list — see xref:guide/exercises.adoc[].

When you create a custom exercise, you give it:

* A name.
* A category — Strength, Cardio, Flexibility, or Custom.
* A muscle group — Chest, Back, Shoulders, Biceps, Triceps, Forearms, Core, Quadriceps, Hamstrings, Glutes, Calves, Full Body, or Cardio.
* An equipment type — Barbell, Dumbbell, Machine, Cable, Bodyweight, Kettlebell, Resistance Band, Cardio Machine, or Other.

You can edit a custom exercise's name, category, muscle group, or equipment type at any time — look for the edit icon next to it in the exercise picker. Built-in exercises cannot be edited.

== Editing and deleting a logged set

Tap any logged set to reopen it in an edit dialog, where you can correct the weight, reps, RPE, or warm-up status. Press and hold a logged set to delete it — there is no confirmation prompt, so treat it as immediate.

== Warm-up sets and the warm-up ramp

Turning on the *Warm-up* toggle before logging a set (covered in xref:first-workout.adoc[]) marks it as a warm-up. Warm-up sets are excluded from your working-set history, volume totals, and personal record checks.

For exercises using loadable equipment — barbell, dumbbell, kettlebell, cable, or machine — RepFoundry can also generate a warm-up ramp for you. Enter the weight you plan to work up to, then use the warm-up ramp option to see a suggested sequence of lighter sets building up to that weight, with the reps decreasing as the weight increases. For barbell exercises, the ramp opens with an empty-bar set. Confirm to add every step in the ramp as warm-up sets in one go, or dismiss and log your own warm-ups manually. The ramp is not offered for bodyweight, resistance band, cardio machine, or other non-loadable equipment.

[[plate-calculator]]
== Plate calculator

For a barbell exercise, a *Plates* action sits next to *Add RPE*, the warm-up pill, and *Add Warm-up* on the set entry card. Tap it to open a plate calculator for the weight you've typed into the weight field — or, if that field is empty, the same suggested or last-logged weight the warm-up ramp falls back to.

The sheet shows your target weight and bar weight (for example, "100kg · bar 20kg"), then a *Per side* breakdown listing the plates to load on each side, largest first — for example "25kg × 1" then "15kg × 1". If your available plates can't hit the target exactly, it shows the closest weight you can load instead, such as "Closest you can load: 100kg (1kg short)". If the target is exactly the bar, it says "Just the bar"; if it's lighter than the bar itself, it says so — "Lighter than the 20kg bar".

Bar weight and which plate sizes you have available are configured under xref:guide/settings.adoc#plate-calculator[Settings → Plate calculator].

== Pyramid sets

For exercises using loadable equipment — barbell, dumbbell, kettlebell, cable, or machine — a *Pyramid* action sits next to *Add RPE*, the warm-up pill, and *Add Warm-up* on the set entry card. Tap it to open a pyramid sheet for the weight you've typed into the weight field — or, if that field is empty, the same suggested or last-logged weight the warm-up ramp falls back to.

The sheet previews an ascending pyramid: five sets, with reps stepping down from 12 to 4 as the weight climbs to your working weight on the final set. For example, working up to 100 kg on a barbell gives 77.5 kg × 12, 82.5 kg × 10, 90 kg × 8, 95 kg × 6, and 100 kg × 4.

Weights are rounded to what you can actually load. For a barbell, that's the bar plus pairs of your smallest available plate — both taken from xref:guide/settings.adoc#plate-calculator[Settings → Plate calculator]; for other equipment, steps of 2.5 kg (or 5 lb, matching your weight unit).

Tapping *Use pyramid* doesn't log anything. Instead, it makes the pyramid the exercise's suggested sets, so the set entry card fills in each set's weight and reps in turn, with the sets you haven't reached yet shown as dimmed suggested rows — the same way sets suggested from your last session normally appear. If you've already logged sets for the exercise, the pyramid picks up from the next set.

== Supersets

Two exercises in the same session can be linked as a superset so you move between them together. Press and hold an exercise card and choose to link it as a superset, then pick the exercise you want to pair it with. Linked exercises are shown grouped together with a superset indicator; tap the unlink icon on the group to separate them again.

== Personal records

RepFoundry automatically checks every working set (warm-ups are excluded) against your all-time best for that exercise, across four record types:

[cols="1,2"]
|===
| Record type | What it tracks

| Estimated one-rep max
| Your highest estimated single-rep strength for the exercise, calculated from weight and reps together.

| Max weight
| The heaviest weight you have lifted in any working set, whatever the rep count.

| Max reps
| The most reps you have completed in a single set, regardless of weight.

| Max volume
| The highest weight × reps total in a single set.
|===

When a set beats a previous best, a celebration appears on screen with a trophy icon, the record type, and the new value. It dismisses itself after a few seconds, or you can tap it to dismiss early.

== Templates

Templates are reusable workout blueprints — a saved list of exercises with target sets and reps for each — so you do not have to rebuild a session from scratch every time. See xref:guide/templates.adoc[] for the full reference on building and managing them.

=== Starting from a template

From the start of a workout, choose *Start from Template* and pick one from the list. The exercise list is pre-populated from the template, and ghost sets from your last session for each exercise are loaded as usual.

=== Creating a template

You can build a template from scratch — give it a name, add exercises, and set a target number of sets and reps for each, reordering them as needed — or save one directly from a workout you have just finished. When you save a completed workout as a template, the number of sets you logged for each exercise becomes its target sets, and the rep count you used most often becomes its target reps.
