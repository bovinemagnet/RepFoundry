= Workout templates
Paul Snow

:description: How to build, reuse, and manage workout templates in RepFoundry, and how templates differ from programmes.

Open *Templates* from *Settings* to create and manage your templates.

== What is a template?

A template is a single reusable workout — a saved list of exercises, each
with a target number of sets and a target rep count. It has no dates and no
schedule attached to it; it is just a blueprint you can start a workout from
whenever you like.

[TIP]
====
A template is not the same as a xref:guide/programmes.adoc[programme]. A
template is one reusable workout. A programme is a multi-week schedule that
assigns templates to specific days and can automatically progress the
weights over time. Build your templates first, then, if you want a
structured schedule, assign them into a programme.
====

== Creating a template from scratch

. Open *Settings* and tap *Templates*.
. Tap the add button and give the template a name.
. On the template's edit screen, tap *Add Exercise* and choose an exercise
  from the picker (the same picker used when xref:guide/workout.adoc[logging
  a workout]). Each exercise you add starts with a default target of 3 sets
  and 10 reps.
. Adjust the target sets and target reps for each exercise as needed, and
  drag exercises by their handle to reorder them.
. Tap *Save*.

You can reopen a template at any time to keep editing it.

== Creating a template from a finished workout

Rather than building a template from scratch, you can turn a workout you
have already completed into one. Open that workout from your history and
use the *Save as Template* action, then give the new template a name.

RepFoundry derives the template's exercises and targets from what you
actually logged:

* Exercises appear in the template in the order each one first appeared in
  the workout.
* An exercise's target sets is the number of working sets you logged for
  it — warm-up sets are not counted. If every set you logged for that
  exercise was a warm-up, all of them are counted instead.
* An exercise's target reps is the most common rep count among those
  counted sets. If two rep counts are tied, whichever one you logged first
  wins the tie.

You cannot save an empty workout as a template — RepFoundry asks you to log
at least one set first. Once created, the new template behaves exactly like
one built from scratch: you can rename it, edit its exercises and targets,
or delete it.

== Starting a workout from a template

From the start of a workout, choose *Start from Template* and pick one from
the list (see xref:first-workout.adoc[] for the other ways a workout can
start). RepFoundry begins a new workout and adds every exercise from the
template to it, ready for you to log.

Target sets and reps are your plan for the session, not a hard limit —
nothing stops you from logging more or fewer sets than the template
suggests. As with adding any exercise, the weight, reps, and RPE fields are
pre-filled with suggestions from your last session for that exercise, where
one exists.

== Editing and deleting templates

From the templates list you can:

* Tap a template to reopen it and edit its name, exercises, targets, or
  exercise order.
* Duplicate a template, which creates an independent copy you can then
  change without affecting the original — useful for building a variant of
  an existing template.
* Delete a template. You are asked to confirm first, and deletion cannot be
  undone.

Deleting or editing a template does not change any workout you have already
completed from it — past history stays exactly as logged.
